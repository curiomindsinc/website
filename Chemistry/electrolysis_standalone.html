Key ideas

  • Electrolysis uses electricity to break down an ionic compound (the electrolyte). It must be molten or dissolved so the ions can move.
  • PANIC: Positive is Anode, Negative Is Cathode.
  • Opposites attract: cations (+) move to the cathode, anions (−) move to the anode.
  • OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons). Reduction at the cathode, oxidation at the anode.
  • Electrons flow through the wires. Ions carry the charge through the liquid — electrons never swim through it.
  • In solutions, cathode: a metal forms only if it is less reactive than hydrogen, otherwise H₂. Anode: a halogen forms if a halide ion is present, otherwise O₂.
