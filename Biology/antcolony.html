The queen is dead

A raider reached the royal chamber. Nothing in this colony can replace her — there is no second queen, and no worker can be fed into one.

No more eggs will be laid. The brood already in the nurseries will hatch, those callows will harden and go to work, and then the colony will simply run down as its ants wear out. About fifteen minutes, and there will be nothing left.

The ants do not know. A real colony finds out over days, by the smell of her going out of the nest. Nothing here will change its behaviour, sound an alarm, or try to do anything about it — you are the only one watching the number fall.

Summon

A dozen is a probe. Sixty is a rout — enough to destroy the deep defence, and the only size that can reach the queen.

A raid is a fight, the nest knows it is happening, and it costs: raiders kill what gets in their way and carry brood back out of the hole. Kollasmosoma sentum is not a fight: it hovers a few millimetres up, picks a worker walking home with food, and puts an egg in its gaster in a twentieth of a second. Nobody sounds an alarm, because there is nothing left to sound one at. The stung ant gets up and goes back to work — and wears out three times as fast. About three minutes later the larva finishes it, and whatever is left is carried to the rubbish tip by a nestmate. Turn on Show parasitized in the View panel to see who is carrying one, and keep an eye on the tip.

The colony has no map

These ants can see, but their world is blurry. They don't rely on sight to find food or navigate their lives. Instead, each ant wanders, follows and leaves scent trails, responding to simple cues around it.

Nobody is in charge, not even the queen, whose main role is to lay eggs.

Everything you are about to watch emerges from these simple actions, and nothing else.
